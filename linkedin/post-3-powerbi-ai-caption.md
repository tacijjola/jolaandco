## Option A — the one I'd post (honest field report + a real insight)

"Type a sentence, get a finished Power BI report."

That's the promise in every AI demo right now. So I spent an evening testing it properly — Microsoft's new agentic workflow, where an AI agent writes the report straight into your Power BI project files.

Here's the honest, unglamorous version. It split cleanly into two halves:

✅ **The data model — flawless.**
From one plain-English prompt, the AI built the entire semantic model: the tables, the relationships between them, and every DAX measure — revenue, margin, cost-per-lead, conversion. Correct. In seconds. The tedious, genuinely hard part — done.

⚠️ **The report itself — not yet.**
When it came to drawing the actual charts, the wheels came off. The AI wrote them in a newer file format than my installed Power BI could read. "Failed to load report." Crashes. A blank canvas. Preview software held together with tape.

And that's the useful bit, so here it is plainly:

→ AI-assisted BI has quietly crossed a line on the **modelling layer** — schemas, measures, structure. That work is logical and text-like, and AI is already good at it.

→ The **report layer** — layout, visuals, the craft of making data readable — is still yours. And will be for a while.

So the near-term win isn't "AI builds your dashboards."
It's "AI clears the modelling grunt-work, so your time goes to the thinking and the design."

Twenty years in operations taught me to tell a real time-saver from a shiny one. This one's real — just not where the hype is pointing.

If you lead a data or BI team: worth testing now, in a sandbox. Not because it's finished — it isn't — but because the half that works is already worth having.

## Why this one works (for the senior audience)
- **Non-hype = trust.** Senior data/BI people are tired of AI hype. An honest "here's what broke" earns more credibility than a polished win.
- **A genuine insight, not just a story:** the *model layer vs report layer* split is a real, useful framing they'll nod at — and may quote.
- **Your unfair advantage is baked in:** "20 years in operations → I can tell a real time-saver from a shiny one." No other "I tried an AI tool" post can say that.
- **Ends with a question,** not a pitch — invites the seniors to weigh in, which is what starts conversations with them.
